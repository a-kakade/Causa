Raw data in `data/raw/olist/` was never modified — verified by the fact every
canonical row count below either equals its raw source exactly (facts/dims) or is
explained by a documented, non-zero-filling absence rule (aggregates). All numbers

## 1. Final analytical window

**2017-01 through 2018-08** (20 months), covering 99,092 of 99,441 orders
(99.65%). Determined by a data-driven rule (order volume < 10% of the median
nonzero-month order count, cross-validated against items/delivery/review coverage
— see `docs/ANALYTICAL_WINDOW.md`), not copied from the prior EDA session.
Implemented as the `fact_orders.in_analytical_window` boolean flag — **no rows
were deleted**; excluded-period orders remain fully queryable.

## 2. Canonical table list

`dim_customer`, `dim_product`, `dim_seller`, `fact_orders`, `fact_order_items`,
`fact_payments`, `fact_reviews`, `agg_order_items`, `agg_order_payments`,
`agg_order_reviews` — 10 tables, all in `data/processed/*.parquet`.

## 3. Grain of each table

| Table | Grain |
|---|---|
| dim_customer | 1 row per customer_id (order-scoped; NOT deduplicated to person-level) |
| dim_product | 1 row per product_id |
| dim_seller | 1 row per seller_id |
| fact_orders | 1 row per order_id (all orders, no filtering) |
| fact_order_items | 1 row per (order_id, order_item_id) |
| fact_payments | 1 row per (order_id, payment_sequential) |
| fact_reviews | 1 row per raw review record (review_row_id surrogate PK) |
| agg_order_items | 1 row per order with ≥1 item |
| agg_order_payments | 1 row per order with ≥1 payment |
| agg_order_reviews | 1 row per order with ≥1 review |

Full field-by-field detail: `docs/CANONICAL_DATA_MODEL.md`.

## 4. Revenue definition

**CAUSA_REVENUE := `agg_order_items.item_price_total`** = `SUM(order_items.price)`
grouped by order, pre-aggregated before any join outward. Chosen over
`SUM(order_payments.payment_value)` because it is decomposable (Price × Volume ×
Mix) and because the two figures' 0.39% disagreement (381 of 98,665 orders,
99.61% match rate) is concentrated in credit-card orders with active installment
plans (0 mismatches at 0 installments, rising through installment buckets) —
consistent with financing interest, not a data defect in either source. Full
reconciliation: `docs/KPI_SEMANTICS_PREVIEW.md`.

## 5. Review governance decision

Use-case-specific, not one global rule: `fact_reviews` retains **all** 99,224 raw
rows (no dedup) for future retrieval use cases; `agg_order_reviews` materializes
both a true aggregate (`avg_review_score`, zero bias by construction) and a single
representative value (`latest_review_score`, by latest `review_answer_timestamp`,
bias −0.0734 vs. the simple average). `highest_review_score` was quantitatively
evaluated and **rejected** for KPI use — its +0.3763 bias and 0% downward-biased
rows show it systematically cherry-picks the best score for multi-review orders.
Full comparison: `docs/REVIEW_GOVERNANCE.md`.

## 6. Customer identity decision

`customer_id` (order-scoped, 99,441 distinct) and `customer_unique_id`
(person-level, 96,096 distinct) are both preserved as separate columns in
`dim_customer`; neither collapses into the other. `fact_orders` denormalizes
`customer_unique_id` for convenience, verified consistent with `dim_customer` by
an automated test. Order-level analysis uses `customer_id`; customer-level/repeat-
purchase analysis must explicitly `groupby("customer_unique_id")`.

## 7. Treatment of incomplete orders

| Structural gap | Count | Representation |
|---|---|---|
| Orders with no order_items | 775 | Absent from `agg_order_items` (not zero-filled); `fact_orders.has_items == False` |
| Orders with no payment | 1 | Absent from `agg_order_payments`; `fact_orders.has_payment == False` |
| Orders with no review | 768 | Absent from `agg_order_reviews`; `fact_orders.has_review == False` |

No zero, no empty-string, no sentinel value was ever substituted for a genuinely
missing fact. Absence is represented by absence from the relevant `agg_*` table,
detectable via the `has_*` boolean flags on `fact_orders`.

## 8. Delivery metric rules

`delivery_days`, `carrier_days`, `delivery_delay_days` computed as documented
timestamp differences; NULL propagates from missing timestamps (never coerced to
0). `delivery_data_quality_flag` ∈ {VALID (96,310), MISSING_CUSTOMER_DATE (2,964),
INVALID_SEQUENCE (166), MISSING_CARRIER_DATE (1)}, priority order as listed.
**Negative-duration investigation (required by this task, not skipped):** all 166
`INVALID_SEQUENCE` orders have a negative `carrier_days` (never negative
`delivery_days`); 121 of 166 (73%) are within 1 hour of zero (plausible clock-sync
noise between subsystems), 2 are multi-day outliers including one 171-day
discrepancy on a single order, most plausibly a genuine data-entry error. These 166
rows were **not removed** — they remain in `fact_orders`, flagged. Full detail:
`docs/CANONICAL_DATA_MODEL.md` §Delivery fields.

## 9. Category handling

`category_translation` joined to `products` with a **LEFT** join (row count
verified unchanged: 32,951 in, 32,951 out). `category_resolution_status` ∈
{TRANSLATED (32,328), NULL_CATEGORY (610, source category itself null),
UNTRANSLATED (13, category present but no translation row — 2 distinct category
names: `pc_gamer`, `portateis_cozinha_e_preparadores_de_alimentos`)}.
`category_name_en` is NULL (not a placeholder string) for the latter two states.

## 10. Geolocation decision

**Not joined into the canonical layer.** `dim_customer`/`dim_seller` already carry
clean `_state`/`_city` fields sufficient for every segmentation scenario identified
so far; `geolocation`'s lack of a usable key (26.18% exact duplicates, no
single/composite PK) makes it unsafe to join without a prior aggregation step that
was not needed and therefore not built. Full reasoning and the aggregation
recipe to use if it's needed later: `docs/GEOLOCATION_DECISION.md`.

## 11. Fan-out protection

Structural: no `fact_orders` field is a summed cross-table measure — revenue,
payment totals, and review scores exist only in the `agg_*` tables, each built by
exactly one `groupby(order_id)` against exactly one raw table, never a join of two
multi-row tables followed by a sum. Proven, not just asserted: `tests/test_fanout.py`
actually performs the naive `order_items ⋈ order_payments` join, confirms it
inflates the total relative to `agg_order_items` (reproducing the same concrete
example order, `03ecec245220b63fd7f68c1737ba99ba`, found in both Step 1 passes),
and confirms that re-joining the already-aggregated `agg_order_items` to
`fact_payments`/`fact_reviews` and correctly taking one row per order reproduces
the exact baseline — while naively re-summing after that same join does not.

## 16. Remaining unresolved issues

- **The 90 "payment < items" revenue mismatches** (the minority direction, 90 of
  381 mismatched orders) are **not** explained by the financing-interest
  hypothesis that explains the majority direction — not individually investigated
  in this pass.
- **The 2 multi-day `INVALID_SEQUENCE` outliers** (including the 171-day one) have
  not been traced to a root cause beyond "plausible data-entry error" — no
  corrective action taken, per instructions, but the *why* remains open.
- **`geolocation` aggregation method is undecided** (median vs. mean vs. mode) —
  deferred until a concrete need arises, per `docs/GEOLOCATION_DECISION.md`.
- **City-name normalization** (casing/accent consistency across
  `customer_city`/`seller_city`) was not audited in Step 2 — flagged but not
  characterized quantitatively.
- **`order_status` handling per KPI** (e.g., whether `canceled`/`unavailable`
  orders should be excluded from `agg_order_items`-based revenue) is intentionally
  **not** decided here — `agg_order_items` includes every order with ≥1 item
  regardless of status; a Step 3 KPI engine must apply any status filter
  explicitly, since this document only defines the raw aggregate, not a
  status-filtered "revenue KPI."
- **`payment_types` field format** (`"credit_card,voucher"` string) is a
  convenience representation, not a normalized structure — a future consumer
  needing to query by individual payment type should split on comma, not assume a
  fixed cardinality.
